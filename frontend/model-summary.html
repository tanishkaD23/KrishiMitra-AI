Agronomic Insights & Technical Takeaways

Why autoregressive persistence dominated tabular ensembles on district wheat data.

1. High Spatial-Temporal Autocorrelation

District-level agricultural yields in Western Uttar Pradesh are anchored by slow-moving structural variables: soil texture (alluvial loam), tubewell density, canal access, and seed varieties. The Previous-Year Baseline ($Y_t \approx Y_{t-1}$) capitalizes on this stability, achieving an outstanding MAE of 0.1892 t/ha.

2. Tree Ensemble Temporal Drift

Random Forest and Extra Trees experienced negative $R^2$ scores on the 2021–2022 test years. Without lag features, standard decision trees cannot extrapolate into years with unprecedented March heat spikes or unseasonal rainfall, leading to high out-of-distribution error.

3. Linear Regularization Breakdown

Ridge Regression showed substantial error ($R^2 = -3.71$, MAE = 0.8148 t/ha). Linear assumptions fail to accommodate agronomic thresholds—such as terminal heat stress when temperatures exceed 32°C in March or nitrogen saturation plateaus.

4. Hybrid Production Architecture

For KrishiMitra's production inference, we utilize the Previous-Year Baseline as the core anchoring prior, overlaid with a localized agronomic residual model adjusting for farmer-specific plot size, sowing window, and March temperature anomalies.

Governance & Data Integrity Safeguards

  • No Deterministic Leakage: Production (Tonnes) was strictly excluded from training and evaluation features to avoid circular calculation ($Yield = Production / Area$).
  • Temporal Independence: No randomized k-fold cross-validation was used across years, eliminating forward-looking data leakage.
  • Plot-Level Limitation: District-level APY census data operates as a regional benchmark; plot-specific variances require on-field agronomic calibration.
